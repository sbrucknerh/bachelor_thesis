\paragraph{Time-respecting paths and edge ordering.}
A directed time-respecting path follows edges in both their transfer direction
and their temporal order \citep{kovanen2011temporal}. With distinct timestamps,
a path from $v_0$ to $v_k$ is a sequence of the form
\[
    (v_0,v_1,t_1),\ldots,(v_{k-1},v_k,t_k),
    \qquad t_1 < \cdots < t_k.
\]
For example, a transfer
$A \to B$ at 10:00 followed by $B \to C$ at 10:05 forms a time-respecting path
from $A$ to $C$. Reversing the times leaves the same static edges, but removes
this path. The first ordering permits a relay interpretation, although it does
not prove that the same funds were forwarded.

Transfers sharing a block timestamp need an additional ordering rule, since the
timestamp alone does not determine which came first. The treatment of these ties
is described in Section~\ref{sec:motif_extraction}. Candidate temporal patterns
are not restricted to paths. Their edges must form a connected graph when edge
directions are ignored, but a star with only incoming transfers is also a valid
candidate structure \citep{paranjape2017motifs}.

\paragraph{The time window $\delta$.}
Temporal order alone does not limit how far apart the transfers may occur.
This thesis follows the total-span condition of \citet{paranjape2017motifs}.
For a candidate sequence of $l$ edges, the first and last timestamps must satisfy
\[
    t_l - t_1 \leq \delta.
\]
The bound applies to the whole sequence, not separately to each gap between
successive edges.

A sliding window enforces this bound as the ordered transfers are processed.
For a new transfer at time $t$, earlier transfers before $t-\delta$ can no
longer form a valid candidate ending at that transfer. The interval
$[t-\delta,t]$ therefore moves with the event sequence. These windows overlap
rather than dividing the history into separate fixed bins. A candidate may
cross an hour or day boundary as long as its total span remains within
$\delta$ \citep{paranjape2017motifs}.

Small windows restrict candidates to short bursts, while larger windows admit
interactions spread across days or weeks. No single interval is assumed to
suit every behaviour. This thesis compares nine windows from five minutes to
30 days, with the grid given in Section~\ref{sec:temporal_window_sweep}.
The window is selected on development data without using the held-out test
results, as described in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation_protocol}. Its value limits
the span of a three-transfer candidate, not the duration of a malicious campaign.
